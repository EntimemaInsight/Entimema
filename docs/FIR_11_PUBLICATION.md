## Methodology and reading architecture

The opening contrasts a reconciled 68-page, 94-KPI pack with unanswered CFO decisions. The executive thesis, four-group information framework, four-level KPI hierarchy, six-layer pack and five findings support scanning. Comparative context, materiality, evidence, ownership and readiness provide the practitioner depth without separate duplicated summaries.

The method is validated data → decision-specific measures → material exceptions → evidence-linked findings → owned actions. Statutory reporting and internal management structures retain different purposes but a common evidence foundation. Actual, budget, pre-period forecast, prior period, run rate and scenario each have a separate analytical role.

- Decision architecture: Objective → Financial outcome → Operational driver → Material exception and interpretation → Decision → Action → Follow-up. Five analytical layers occupy four existing responsive framework groups.
- KPI hierarchy: Enterprise Outcomes → Financial Mechanisms → Operational Drivers → Diagnostic Evidence. Definition governance and the DSO example preserve interpretation limits and downward evidence paths.
- Pack architecture: Executive Decision Page; Performance Bridge; Cash and Financial Position; Business Drivers; Forecast and Scenarios; Actions and Evidence.
- Finding anatomy: Observation → Driver → Evidence → Implication → Action → Owner. Magnitude, uncertainty, timing, approval and completion evidence remain explicit.
- Materiality is decision-specific; attention thresholds do not waive reconciliation. Favourable results require sustainability tests.
- Commentary distinguishes facts, calculations, supported explanations and unresolved hypotheses. Revisions invalidate dependent narrative.
- Twenty compact failure/control rows address false completeness, stale commentary, unowned actions, lost evidence and inappropriate reporting cadence.

## Reconciled fictional example

H1 means January–June 2026; positions are at 30 June. The 8 July forecast covers the full year and December positions, visibly separated from H1 comparisons.

- Revenue: budget EUR 30.0m; actual EUR 31.8m; FY forecast EUR 63.0m.
- Product inputs: Core 200,000 units at EUR 100 budget and EUR 96.5 actual; Specialist 50,000 budget and 62,500 actual units at EUR 200. Budget gross margins are 32% and 26% respectively.
- Revenue bridge: volume +1.5m, mix +1.0m, price −0.7m = +1.8m. Volume is 83.3% of the net upside.
- Gross-profit bridge: 9.0000 + 0.4500 + 0.2000 − 0.7000 − 0.1200 − 0.0532 = 8.7768m. Actual margin is 27.6%, down 2.4 percentage points. Positive absolute mix does not mean favourable margin mix: standard-economics margin falls to 29.692%.
- Actual operating costs of 5.6768m produce EBITDA 3.1m against 3.6m budget. The 0.15m maintenance underspend is a timing benefit, with H2 catch-up included once in the forecast.
- Operating cash: 3.1 − 1.4 − 1.5 + 1.0 − 0.5 = 0.7m, against 2.8m planned. Additional working-capital absorption of 1.6m plus the EBITDA miss of 0.5m explains the 2.1m shortfall.
- Net debt: opening 7.0 + actual capex 3.9 − operating cash 0.7 = 10.2m. Budget 7.0 + 3.8 − 2.8 = 8.0m.
- Debt/cash roll-forward: opening 9.8/2.8m becomes 12.0/1.8m after 2.2m net borrowing. No dividends, currency effects or restricted cash are assumed.
- FY net debt: 7.0 + capex 6.4 − operating cash 2.9 = 10.5m. H2 requirements are revenue 31.2m, EBITDA 2.9m and cash 2.2m.
- FY available liquidity: 14.0m committed facility − 12.0m debt + 1.5m free cash = 3.5m, only 0.5m above the fictional 3.0m board minimum. A 0.8m collection delay reduces this to 2.7m; a feasible 0.6m capex deferral restores 3.3m. Interim troughs still need review.
- DSO/DIO use average balances and explicitly stated 181-day H1 and 365-day forecast conventions; they are not substituted for closing-balance movements.

### Five executive findings

1. Growth does not convert to budgeted earnings: Commercial proposes targeted repricing by 15 July; CFO approval and August realised-contribution review follow.
2. Material price and usage reduce earnings, but usage causality is unresolved: Operations validates production evidence by 17 July; Procurement owns sourcing; Controller reviews evidence before forecast revision.
3. Receivables and inventory absorb cash: Working Capital Lead agrees collections and stock-build limits by 14 July, with weekly realised-cash follow-up.
4. Maintenance underspend is not sustainable savings: Operations confirms timing by 17 July and FP&A retains the catch-up once, with completion evidence.
5. Reduced liquidity cushion requires protective decisions: Treasurer tests weekly troughs by 16 July; CFO decides whether the capex contingency is feasible, with weekly headroom review.

Decision-ready for evidenced targeted repricing; usage explanation review-required; liquidity base case ready with limitations for protective contingency planning. Final forecast approval awaits collection-timing and weekly-trough review.
